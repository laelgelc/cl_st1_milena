I knew I was smiling without meaning to when I pressed the blade to his throat with one hand, the other pressed to his mouth, both gloved, prepared, feeling his teeth biting at my palm through the fabric, his underbite reaching for my wrist, but I'd held him, kneeling on his chest, his heart thundering under my kneecaps, and his blood heating up under my calves. I’d had him just where I wanted him to be, feeling that same helplessness he’d held over my family for the past two years of torture.

    He wasn’t smiling this time, wasn’t laughing with his smoke-laden voice and his balmed lips, no, now he was the one fighting without a chance in the world to make it out, because that’s how it’s set up. Give you something you’re sure you can work out over the next couple months when things are better, just a little bit, but then they raise the prices, move the goalpost. Same as he thought he could go for my hands when I’d pushed him down, then my legs when I’d kneeled over him, my chest when I’d cupped his throat, always moving, always looking for a way out, always hopeless.
