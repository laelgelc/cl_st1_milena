    What if, in the trans-fiction trope of the egg meeting their adult self, the adult needed just as much help as the egg did?

    What if transitioning didn’t solve everything?

    August Hagemann is in Wisconsin in 2004, and struggling to find safety or comfort in his family or friends during his senior year of high school.

    Autumn Hagemann is in California in 2024, and failing to find internal peace despite accepting that they are trans and starting estrogen.

    And every night, both of them dream of a cavernous, frozen cave - a space where they both get chances to talk to the only person who can figure out what is wrong: themselves.

    **************

    Content Warnings: The Girl Beneath the Ice is not a hopeless novel, but it deals with serious mental health issues. Depression, dysphoria, and suicidal ideation are present in every chapter.

    Internalized transphobia and homophobia are also pervasive, as are homophobic/transphobic slurs and self-loathing diatribes.

    There are brief depictions of self harm.

    Please use caution while reading and take breaks if necessary.
