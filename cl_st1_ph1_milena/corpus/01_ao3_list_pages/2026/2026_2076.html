If he looked up to the heavens, he would learn of the cosmos and master the universe. If he looked down to the earth, he would learn of his world and uncover the secrets of men—but only just that.

    The human—the fool—looks down. Minkyu, fox-cursed, does the same.

    I’m going to kill him, he thinks. He would say it out loud, but he can’t—the paralytic chewing through his nerves keeps him docile, and willpower only gets him so far, only enough to dry the damp burn in his eyes.

    He can’t feel the rods prodding in his skull—the brain lacks such receptors because it isn’t meant to be touched—but he feels when one precise stab triggers an ugly spasm across his face. Self-loathing comes swift and relentless, and Minkyu’s canines, grown sharp with his anger, puncture the swollen bruise of his lower lip. Blood wells, but somehow, the shame still stings more.

    I’m going to fucking kill him.

    But that’s a lie, of course, because Minkyu knows there’s no way he could possibly kill Noskov, not now or ever.
