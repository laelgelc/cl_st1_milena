    Summary

    There was a time when anger felt like oxygen.

    Every word sparked.
    Every silence smoked.

    I learned early how to breathe in heat
    without coughing.

    This is not the story of flames.
    It is the story of what remains
    after the shouting stops,
    after the doors close,
    after the ash settles into the cracks of the floor.

    Some fires do not consume the house.

    They live in the beams.
    In the marrow.
    In the quiet red glow beneath the ribs.

    I used to think the only choices were
    to burn
    or to burn out.

    But embers are patient things.

    They do not leap.
    They do not beg to be seen.
    They wait.

    And if left untended,
    they cool.

    If guarded too fiercely,
    they scorch the hands that hold them.

    So I am learning the difference
    between warmth
    and wildfire.

    If you step inside this house,
    you may not see the smoke anymore.

    But if you press your palm to the walls,
    you’ll feel what lingers
    not destruction,

    just heat
    that once had nowhere else to go.
