    A gentle, slow-burn romance set in late-1940s South Korea, on the eve of the Korean War.

    In the lively wholesale markets of Busan—vibrant ports of trade in a newly liberated nation still under American occupation—shy nineteen-year-old Park Eun Jin accompanies her widowed mother on trips from Gyeongju. Amid the bustle of vendors and goods from foreign trade, she steals quiet glances at a kind young soldier, Kang Ji Woon, who helps elderly women by carrying heavy sacks and lending his strength before sitting to eat.

    Shaped by loss—Eun Jin raised alone after her father's early death as a soldier, Ji Woon honoring his martyred father's legacy—both see duty as their path: she quietly shelves dreams of poetry for household survival; he believes military life offers only sacrifice.
